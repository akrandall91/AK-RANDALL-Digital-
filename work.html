The method behind the work

Start with the real decision—not the requested deliverable.

A website, dashboard, operating system, or planning tool becomes valuable when it helps someone understand the problem and move forward with confidence.

  1. 01Find the real problem

    Identify where customers, teams, or decision-makers are losing clarity or momentum.

  2. 02Connect the information

    Bring the useful evidence, specialist knowledge, and operating context into one path.

  3. 03Build the experience

    Turn the logic into a usable system, journey, or decision-support product.

  4. 04Make proof understandable

    Show what is verified, assumed, simulated, unfinished, or still dependent on future results.

  5. 05Move toward action

    Give the buyer or team a clearer next decision instead of another disconnected artifact.

Completed transformation

Church of the Redeemer

Turn a difficult website into a clear visitor journey.

The problem
Important information and identity existed, but mobile visitors and first-time guests had to work too hard to understand the church and choose a next step.
What was built
A responsive redesign organized around real visitor questions, clearer page hierarchy, accessible navigation, stronger storytelling, and action-focused pathways.
Why it matters
When people can orient themselves quickly, the organization has a stronger foundation for visits, participation, giving, and future measurement.
What it proves
AKRD can uncover the value already inside an organization and build a digital experience that makes that value easier to understand and act on.

Useful for: organizations with a dated website, confusing navigation, scattered information, or a customer journey that no longer reflects who they are.

AKRD Business Command Center

Keep the customer story intact across every handoff.

The problem
Lead details, pricing, project delivery, invoices, follow-up, and reporting can live in separate tools, forcing an owner to rebuild context before making the next decision.
What was built
A working owner workspace connecting lead management, qualification, quoting, pipeline, projects, invoicing, renewals, reports, and website activity.
Why it matters
A connected operation can reduce searching, repeated entry, missed follow-up, and decisions based on partial information.
What it proves
AKRD can translate the way a business actually works into a practical operating system rather than adding another isolated application.

Useful for: service businesses, contractors, consultants, and growth teams whose customer and delivery information is scattered across tools or people.

Demonstration boundary: all public companies, contacts, opportunities, transactions, and performance figures are fictional. They demonstrate working product behavior—not AKRD or customer results.

In development / Active civic build

Greensboro SEP Tracker

Make long-term public progress easier to follow.

The problem
A long-term public plan, annual reports, funding records, votes, permits, and outside evidence were available, but answering “what changed?” still required substantial research.
What is working now
An active tracker matching 21 goals across three reporting years, with citations, reporting gaps, outside checks, collaboration views, and a sourced correction path.
Why it matters
Residents, board members, staff, and decision-makers can inspect a clearer record without manually rebuilding the history from separate documents.
What it proves
AKRD can structure scattered public evidence into a transparent reporting system while keeping source boundaries, uncertainty, privacy, and open work visible.

Useful for: agencies, boards, nonprofits, grant programs, and public-facing initiatives whose commitments and results accumulate across years and sources.

Civic-work boundary: Andrew serves on the Greensboro Community Sustainability Council. He developed this tracker independently; the board and City did not commission it, and it is not a paid AKRD client engagement.

Private planning product / Illustrative scenario

AKRD Lighting Engine

Turn specialist knowledge into a decision buyers can inspect.

The problem
Lighting performance, seasonal energy, battery reserve, civil work, lifecycle cost, carbon, and evidence can be evaluated separately even though the buyer must approve one complete project.
What was built
A working planning product that carries the same site and operating context through measured lighting, energy, alternatives, sensitivity, evidence, and decision-brief screens.
Why it matters
Field solar experience showed that a promising component does not automatically prove the complete project. Buyers need the tradeoffs, assumptions, and limits in one understandable path.
What it proves
AKRD can convert technical expertise and commercial knowledge into a guided product that helps non-specialists evaluate a complex recommendation.

Useful for: technical manufacturers, infrastructure providers, engineering-adjacent teams, and businesses whose expertise is trapped in files, spreadsheets, calculators, or employee knowledge.

Planning boundary: this is an AKRD product, not a paid client engagement. Illustrative inputs show behavior, not customer results. It is not stamped engineering; final projects require manufacturer and qualified professional review.
